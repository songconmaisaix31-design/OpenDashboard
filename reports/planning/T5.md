## Outcome

T5 now defines a self-contained provider-neutral contract for normalized
observations, redacted evidence, provenance, deterministic incident grouping,
deduplication, fixed severity, lifecycle, and stable availability states.

The central decision is to separate service condition from data availability
and incident lifecycle. `unknown`, `stale`, and contract `error` therefore
cannot be interpreted as health or recovery. Incident episodes are derived
from explicit rule versions and evaluation times, resolved episodes never
reopen, and recurrence creates a new deterministic episode.

## Contract choices

- SHA-256 identities over canonical UTF-8 JSON remove arrival-order and
  machine-clock dependence.
- Fixture and live provenance are isolated in grouping fingerprints so mocked
  evidence cannot be silently combined with live claims.
- Conflicting duplicates fail visibly instead of selecting a first or last
  writer.
- Every evaluated observation requires redacted, digest-verified evidence.
- Severity is fixed by the immutable rule version; dynamic scoring is deferred.
- Lifecycle is aggregation-owned and observation-driven:
  `open -> recovering -> resolved`, with a deterministic return from
  `recovering` to `open` when failure recurs.

## Fixture and acceptance coverage

The plan specifies ten bounded fixture cases covering the golden path,
shuffled replay, group separation, unknown and stale data, observation
conflicts, missing evidence, post-resolution recurrence, trust-mode isolation,
and the exact freshness boundary. Twelve acceptance checks cover canonical
output, evidence integrity, provenance, lifecycle safety, and scope isolation.

## Boundaries preserved

No collectors, providers, storage, UI, actions, network access, live
integration, application code, dependency, root configuration, or cross-model
mapping was designed or implemented. The proposal does not modify the active
competition contract and cannot gate T0-T4 work before separate
post-competition reconciliation.

## Assumptions and residual risks

The contract assumes already normalized and redacted records, stable keys, an
immutable rule version, a complete bounded input set, and an explicit
evaluation time. Residual risks are inconsistent future canonicalization,
upstream key instability, redaction failure before the boundary, pessimistic
stale-state freezing, fixed-severity limitations, and deferred late-event or
cross-source reconciliation.
